# Thinking Pattern 028 — Waiting & Passing Time

## Hindi Thinking Pattern

Waiting is a common part of everyday life—at home, outside, or while meeting someone.

Representative Hindi thoughts:

- थोड़ा इंतज़ार करो।
- बस दो मिनट।
- अभी आता हूँ।
- कितना समय लगेगा?
- अभी तक नहीं आए?
- लाइन लंबी है।
- मेरी बारी कब आएगी?
- समय कैसे बिताएँ?
- अभी समय है।
- जल्दी क्या है?
- थोड़ा और रुकते हैं।
- चलो बैठते हैं।
- इंतज़ार करना पड़ेगा।
- अभी नहीं, बाद में।
- चलो तब तक चाय पी लेते हैं।

**Core intention**

Talk naturally about waiting, delays, and passing time without sounding impatient.

---

## Natural English Expressions

| Expression | Register | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Just give me a minute. | Casual | Short wait |
| I'll be right there. | Casual | Arriving soon |
| It won't be long. | Neutral | Reassuring someone |
| We might have to wait a bit. | Neutral | Delay |
| Let's wait here. | Neutral | Choosing to wait |
| We've got some time. | Casual | No hurry |

---

## Nuance

English speakers often soften delays with reassuring phrases such as *It won't be long* or *I'll be right there*. These sound more natural than repeatedly saying "Wait."

## Common Mistakes

❌ Wait only two minutes.

✅ Just give me two minutes.

**Why?** "Just give me..." is the natural reassurance.

---

❌ I am coming now.

✅ I'll be right there.

**Why?** Native speakers often use this fixed expression.

---

❌ Time is there.

✅ We've got plenty of time.

**Why?** English describes having time, not time "being there."

---

## Indian English vs Natural English

| Common Indian English | Natural English |
|---|---|
| Wait only two minutes. | Just give me two minutes. |
| I am coming now. | I'll be right there. |
| Time is there. | We've got plenty of time. |

## Similar Expressions

| Expression | Best used when |
|---|---|
| Just give me a minute. | Short delay |
| I'll be right there. | Approaching someone |
| It won't be long. | Reassurance |
| We've got some time. | No rush |

---

## Memory Tip

When someone has to wait, English usually focuses on **reassurance** rather than simply telling them to wait.

## Quick Recap

- **Most useful:** I'll be right there.
- **Safest:** Just give me a minute.
- **Avoid:** "I am coming now."
- **Key takeaway:** Reassure people while they wait instead of giving direct commands.
