# Thinking Pattern 020 — Park Activities & Public Spaces

## Hindi Thinking Pattern

Parks and public spaces involve everyday activities beyond greeting people. Typical Hindi thoughts include:

- यहाँ बैठते हैं।
- थोड़ी देर आराम करते हैं।
- बच्चों को खेलने दो।
- घास पर मत चलो।
- कुत्ते को पट्टे पर रखिए।
- यहाँ बहुत भीड़ है।
- दूसरी बेंच पर चलते हैं।
- पानी की बोतल साथ लाई?
- थोड़ा स्ट्रेच कर लेते हैं।
- धीरे चलिए।
- रास्ता दीजिए।
- यहाँ फोटो लेते हैं।
- चलो अब घर चलते हैं।
- कूड़ा डस्टबिन में डालो।
- पार्क बंद होने वाला है।

**Core intention**

Talk naturally about everyday activities, movement, and etiquette in parks and other public spaces.

---

## Natural English Expressions

| Expression | Register | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Let's sit here. | Casual | Choosing a place |
| Let's take a short break. | Neutral | Resting |
| Watch your step. | Neutral | Safety |
| Please use the bin. | Neutral | Keeping the place clean |
| Excuse me, coming through. | Polite | Passing people |
| Let's head home. | Casual | Leaving the park |

---

## Nuance

English speakers often use short, cooperative expressions in public spaces. Requests are usually softened with **please**, **excuse me**, or **let's**.

## Common Mistakes by Hindi Speakers

❌ Throw the garbage in dustbin.

✅ Put your rubbish in the bin.

**Why?** Native speakers usually say *bin* or *trash can* instead of *dustbin*.

---

❌ Give side.

✅ Excuse me, coming through.

**Why?** This is the natural polite expression.

---

❌ Sit here only.

✅ Let's sit here.

**Why?** Avoid unnecessary "only".

---

## Indian English vs Natural English

| Common Indian English | Natural English |
|---|---|
| Give side. | Excuse me, coming through. |
| Throw it in the dustbin. | Put it in the bin. |
| Sit here only. | Let's sit here. |

## Memory Tip

In public spaces, think about **sharing the space politely**. English relies on short, courteous expressions.
