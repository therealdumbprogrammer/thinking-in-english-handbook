# Thinking Pattern 031 — Household Chores & Shared Responsibilities

## Hindi Thinking Pattern

Running a home means sharing everyday responsibilities.

Representative Hindi thoughts:

- आज बर्तन मैं धो देता हूँ।
- तुम झाड़ू लगा देना।
- कूड़ा किसकी बारी है?
- चलो काम बाँट लेते हैं।
- मैं खाना बना लेता हूँ।
- तुम मेज़ लगा दो।
- कपड़े तह कर दो।
- आज मेरी बारी है।
- साथ में जल्दी हो जाएगा।
- सब अपना-अपना काम कर लो.
- पहले ये काम खत्म करते हैं।
- बाद में आराम करेंगे.

**Core intention**

Coordinate household chores, divide responsibilities, and cooperate naturally.

---

## Natural English Expressions

| Expression | Register | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| I'll do the dishes. | Casual | Taking responsibility |
| Could you sweep the floor? | Neutral | Asking for help |
| Let's split the chores. | Casual | Sharing work |
| It's my turn today. | Neutral | Taking turns |
| I'll take care of it. | Neutral | Reassuring others |
| We can finish it together. | Casual | Working together |

---

## Nuance

Native speakers often frame chores as shared responsibilities rather than commands.

- I'll do the dishes.
- You can vacuum the living room.
- Let's get this done together.

This sounds cooperative instead of bossy.

## Common Mistakes

❌ Do the cleaning.

✅ Do the dishes. / Clean the house.

**Why?** English prefers specific chore names.

❌ Today my chance.

✅ It's my turn today.

**Why?** "Turn" is the natural word.

❌ Divide the work.

✅ Let's split the chores.

**Why?** "Split the chores" is the everyday expression.

## Memory Tip

Think about **sharing responsibility**, not assigning orders. English household conversations often sound collaborative.
