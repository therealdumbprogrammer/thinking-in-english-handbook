# Thinking Pattern 010 — Home Utilities

## Hindi Thinking Pattern

Many everyday conversations at home revolve around electricity, water, gas, and the internet.

Typical Hindi thoughts include:

- लाइट चली गई।
- बिजली कब आएगी?
- पानी नहीं आ रहा।
- पानी का प्रेशर कम है।
- गैस खत्म हो गई।
- नया सिलेंडर बुक करना है।
- वाई-फ़ाई नहीं चल रहा।
- इंटरनेट बहुत धीमा है।
- राउटर रीस्टार्ट कर दो।
- चार्जर लगा है?
- इन्वर्टर चल रहा है।
- एसी बंद कर दो।
- पंखा चला दो।
- गीजर ऑन कर दो।
- पानी की टंकी भर गई क्या?

**Core intention**

Describe common utility problems, ask for simple actions, and coordinate everyday household tasks naturally.

---

## Natural English Expressions

| Expression | Register | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| The power's out. | Neutral | Electricity has gone |
| There's no water. | Neutral | Water supply issue |
| The Wi-Fi isn't working. | Neutral | Internet problem |
| The internet is really slow today. | Neutral | Slow connection |
| We've run out of gas. | Neutral | Cooking gas finished |
| Could you restart the router? | Neutral | Wi-Fi troubleshooting |
| Turn on the fan, please. | Neutral | Asking someone |
| Switch off the AC before you leave. | Neutral | Saving electricity |

---

## Nuance

English speakers usually describe the **current situation** rather than translating the Hindi thought literally.

Instead of saying:

> Current has gone.

they naturally say:

> The power's out.

Likewise:

- The Wi-Fi isn't working.
- We've run out of gas.
- There's no water.

These expressions sound idiomatic and are used in everyday conversation.

## Common Mistakes by Hindi Speakers

❌ Current is gone.

✅ The power's out.

**Why?** Native speakers talk about the power, not "current."

---

❌ Wi-Fi is not coming.

✅ The Wi-Fi isn't working.

**Why?** Networks don't "come"; they work or don't work.

---

❌ Gas finished.

✅ We've run out of gas.

**Why?** This is the natural everyday expression.

| Gas finished. | We've run out of gas. |

## Memory Tip

Describe the **problem first**, then ask for the action.

> The Wi-Fi isn't working. Could you restart the router?

This order sounds natural in everyday English.

## Quick Recap

- **Most useful:** The power's out.
- **Safest:** The Wi-Fi isn't working.
- **Avoid:** "Current is gone."
- **Key takeaway:** Use simple everyday expressions to describe utility problems before suggesting a solution.
