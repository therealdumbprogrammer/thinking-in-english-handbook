# Thinking Pattern 038 — Using Public Facilities

## Hindi Thinking Pattern

Everyday life often involves using public facilities such as public toilets, drinking water stations, ticket machines, waiting areas, escalators, elevators, parking meters and information desks.

Representative Hindi thoughts:

- वॉशरूम कहाँ है?
- पीने का पानी कहाँ मिलेगा?
- यहाँ बैठ सकते हैं?
- लाइन यहीं लगेगी?
- टिकट कहाँ से मिलेगा?
- मशीन काम नहीं कर रही।
- ये सीट खाली है?
- यहाँ मोबाइल चार्ज कर सकते हैं?
- लिफ्ट किधर है?
- एस्केलेटर बंद है।
- रास्ता किधर है?
- यहाँ इंतज़ार करते हैं।
- धन्यवाद।
- माफ़ कीजिए।
- कोई मदद कर सकता है?

**Core intention**

Ask for directions, use shared facilities politely, and handle common situations in public places.

---

## Natural English Expressions

| Expression | Register | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Where's the restroom? | Neutral | Finding facilities |
| Is this seat taken? | Neutral | Public seating |
| Could you point me to the elevator? | Polite | Asking directions |
| Where can I get drinking water? | Neutral | Water station |
| Is this the right queue? | Neutral | Waiting in line |
| Excuse me, could you help me? | Polite | Asking for assistance |

---

## Nuance

English conversations in public spaces are usually brief and courteous. People commonly begin with **Excuse me** before asking a question.

## Common Mistakes

❌ Where is washroom?

✅ Where's the restroom?

**Why?** Include the article and use the common everyday term.

---

❌ Queue is here?

✅ Is this the right queue?

**Why?** Use a complete question.

---

❌ Lift where?

✅ Where's the elevator?

**Why?** Natural English uses standard question order.

## Memory Tip

In public places, start with **Excuse me**, ask one clear question, and thank the other person.

## Quick Recap

- **Most useful:** Excuse me, could you help me?
- **Safest:** Where's the restroom?
- **Avoid:** "Lift where?"
- **Key takeaway:** Keep questions short, polite, and specific when using public facilities.
