# Thinking Pattern 033 — Guests at Home

## Hindi Thinking Pattern

Hosting guests involves welcoming them, making them comfortable, and offering food or drinks.

Representative Hindi thoughts:

- आइए, अंदर आइए।
- बैठिए।
- कैसे आना हुआ?
- कुछ लेंगे?
- चाय या कॉफ़ी?
- पानी ले आइए।
- आराम से बैठिए।
- घर ढूँढ़ने में दिक्कत तो नहीं हुई?
- खाना खाकर जाइए।
- थोड़ा और लीजिए।
- संकोच मत कीजिए।
- अपना ही घर समझिए।
- जल्दी क्या है?
- फिर आइएगा।
- आने के लिए धन्यवाद।

**Core intention**

Welcome guests warmly, offer hospitality naturally, and end the visit politely.

---

## Natural English Expressions

| Expression | Register | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Come on in. | Casual | Inviting someone inside |
| Please have a seat. | Neutral | Welcoming |
| Can I get you something to drink? | Neutral | Offering refreshments |
| Tea or coffee? | Neutral | Offering a choice |
| Make yourself at home. | Friendly | Making guests comfortable |
| Thanks for coming. | Neutral | Ending the visit |

---

## Nuance

English-speaking hosts usually make guests feel comfortable through gentle offers instead of repeated insistence.

For example:

- Can I get you something to drink?
- Please make yourself at home.
- Help yourself if you'd like some more.

These sound warm without being overly forceful.

## Common Mistakes

❌ Take tea.

✅ Would you like some tea?

**Why?** English uses offers rather than commands.

---

❌ Sit.

✅ Please have a seat.

**Why?** This sounds much more welcoming.

---

❌ Come again.

✅ We hope to see you again soon.

**Why?** This is a more natural farewell.

---

## Memory Tip

Think about **making your guest feel comfortable**, not simply serving them. English hospitality relies on warm offers and gentle invitations.

## Quick Recap

- **Most useful:** Make yourself at home.
- **Safest:** Can I get you something to drink?
- **Avoid:** "Take tea."
- **Key takeaway:** Welcome guests with polite offers rather than direct instructions.
