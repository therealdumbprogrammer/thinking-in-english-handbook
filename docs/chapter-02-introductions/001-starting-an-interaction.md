# Thinking Pattern 001 — Starting an Interaction

## Hindi Thinking Pattern

When meeting someone for the first time, Hindi speakers often think:

- नमस्ते बोल दूँ।
- हेलो बोल देता हूँ।
- बात शुरू करूँ?
- कुछ तो बोलना चाहिए।
- पहले विश कर लेता हूँ।
- कैसे शुरुआत करूँ?
- मैं ही बात शुरू करूँ?
- शायद इन्हें भी बात करनी हो।
- अभी बोलना ठीक रहेगा?
- चुप रहना थोड़ा अजीब लग रहा है।

**Core intention**

You are **not** trying to introduce yourself yet.

You simply want to open the conversation politely.

---

## Natural English Expressions

| Expression | Register | When to use |
|------------|----------|-------------|
| Hi! | Casual | Friends, neighbours, classmates |
| Hello! | Neutral | Almost any situation |
| Good morning. | Neutral | Morning meetings, office |
| Good afternoon. | Neutral | Professional settings |
| Good evening. | Neutral | Evening events |
| Hi there! | Casual | Friendly informal interaction |
| Excuse me... | Neutral | Before asking a question |
| Mind if I join you? | Polite | Joining someone |

---

## Nuance

Native English speakers rarely search for a "perfect opening sentence."

Most conversations begin with a simple greeting. The greeting is not meant to impress anyone—it simply signals that you're open to talking.

Hindi speakers sometimes delay speaking because they're trying to remember the "correct English." Native speakers generally value friendliness much more than perfection.

## Real-Life Examples

### Office

**Hindi Thought**

पहले हेलो बोल देता हूँ।

**Natural English**

> Hi, good morning!

---

### Conference

**Hindi Thought**

बात शुरू करूँ?

**Natural English**

> Hi, is this seat taken?

---

### Lift

**Hindi Thought**

पहली बार मिले हैं।

**Natural English**

> Hello! I don't think we've met before. I'm Vivek.

---

### Waiting Area

**Hindi Thought**

कुछ तो बोलना चाहिए।

**Natural English**

> Hi! Have you been waiting long?

---

### Family Gathering

**Hindi Thought**

चलो मिल लेते हैं।

**Natural English**

> Hi! It's nice to finally meet you.

## Common Thinking Traps

❌ **Myself Vivek.**

✅ **Hi, I'm Vivek.**

**Why?**

Native speakers introduce themselves using **I'm...**, not **Myself...**

---

❌ **Good morning dear friend.**

✅ **Good morning!**

**Why?**

English greetings are usually short and natural.

---

❌ **Can I do friendship with you?**

✅ **Hi, I'm Vivek. Nice to meet you.**

**Why?**

Friendships develop naturally. They are rarely announced.

## Similar Expressions

| Expression | Best used when |
|------------|----------------|
| Hi | Everyday conversations |
| Hello | Safe in almost every situation |
| Good morning | Professional morning interactions |
| Hi there | Friendly informal tone |
| Excuse me | Getting someone's attention first |

---

## Memory Tip

Don't think:

> "Which English sentence should I remember?"

Think:

> "I just need to acknowledge the other person."

A simple **Hi** is often enough.

## Quick Recap

- **Most useful:** Hi!
- **Safest:** Hello!
- **Avoid:** "Myself...", overly formal openings, translating Hindi word-for-word.
- **Key takeaway:** Start the interaction simply. The conversation can grow naturally after the greeting.
