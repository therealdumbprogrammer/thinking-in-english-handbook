# Thinking Pattern 004 — Introducing Other People

## Hindi Thinking Pattern

When two people don't know each other, Hindi speakers often think:

- इन दोनों का परिचय करा देता हूँ।
- इन्हें मिलवाना चाहिए।
- ये भी एक-दूसरे के काम आ सकते हैं।
- शायद इनकी अच्छी बनेगी।
- दोनों एक ही क्षेत्र में हैं।
- पहले इनका परिचय करा दूँ।
- मिल लीजिए एक-दूसरे से।
- आप दोनों पहले नहीं मिले?

**Core intention**

Help two people become comfortable by giving enough context for them to start talking.

---

## Natural English Expressions

| Expression | Register | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Have you two met? | Casual | Friends, family |
| I'd like you to meet Rahul. | Neutral | Most situations |
| This is Neha from our design team. | Neutral | Office |
| Let me introduce you to... | Formal | Meetings, events |
| You both work in AI, don't you? | Casual | Creating connection |
| I think you'll get along. | Casual | Friends, networking |

---

## Nuance

Native speakers usually provide **context**, not just names.

Instead of saying only:

> This is Rahul.

they often add one useful detail:

> This is Rahul. We worked together at Amazon.

That gives both people an easy place to continue the conversation.

## Real-Life Conversations

### Office

**Hindi Thought**

इन दोनों का परिचय करा देता हूँ।

> A: Sarah, I'd like you to meet Rahul.
>
> B: Nice to meet you.
>
> C: Nice to meet you too.

---

### Conference

**Hindi Thought**

दोनों एक ही क्षेत्र में हैं।

> A: You both work in cloud engineering. I thought you should meet.
>
> B: Great to meet you.
>
> C: Likewise.

---

### Family Gathering

**Hindi Thought**

पहले मिलवा देता हूँ।

> A: Have you two met before?
>
> B: I don't think so.
>
> A: This is my cousin, Amit.

---

### Friends

**Hindi Thought**

शायद इनकी अच्छी बनेगी।

> A: I think you two have a lot in common.
>
> B: Really?
>
> C: Let's find out.

---

### Customer Visit

**Hindi Thought**

इनसे मिलिए।

> A: Let me introduce you to our project manager.
>
> B: Pleasure to meet you.
>
> C: Likewise.

## Common Thinking Traps

❌ Meet with my friend.

✅ I'd like you to meet my friend.

**Why?** "Meet with" means to have a meeting. Here you're introducing people.

---

❌ He is my childhood friend only.

✅ He's a childhood friend of mine.

**Why?** Avoid adding "only" for emphasis.

---

❌ Both are same field.

✅ You both work in the same field.

**Why?** Complete the sentence naturally.

---

## Similar Expressions

| Expression | Best used when |
|---|---|
| This is... | Quick introductions |
| I'd like you to meet... | Polite introductions |
| Let me introduce you to... | Formal settings |
| Have you two met? | Checking before introducing |

---

## Memory Tip

Don't just exchange names. Give one small piece of context that helps the conversation continue.

## Quick Recap

- **Most useful:** I'd like you to meet...
- **Safest:** Let me introduce you to...
- **Avoid:** Literal translations like "Meet with my friend."
- **Key takeaway:** A good introduction includes a name and a little context.
